# World rules

Examine one rule the author names. Explore its consequences before they commit to it.

## Gather

- Read the rule as the author states it, and every established use of it: encyclopedia entries through the MCP server (`get-encyclopedia-entry`) and the chapters where it appears.
- Note the author's stated intent for the rule, if given.

## Examine

Answer each question from canon first, then from inference:

- Prerequisites: who or what can use it, and what it takes to qualify.
- Cost: what it consumes, and who pays.
- Limits: range, duration, scale, frequency. What it cannot do.
- Exceptions: when it fails, who is immune, what overrides it.
- Consequences: what institutions, trade, war, families and daily life would look like if the rule held. Who would exploit it, and how.

## Report

- Two sections: "Established" (quote the entry or passage for every claim) and "Implications" (your reasoning, labelled as speculation).
- Where the manuscript and the encyclopedia differ, report both; the continuity-check skill owns the contradiction.
- End with the open questions the author must decide, not with decisions.

## Never

- Present an inference as canon.
- Write encyclopedia text or add rules the author has not chosen.
- Rewrite scenes to fit the rule.
